\begin{abstract}
This document introduces the charging behaviour of a resistor-capacitor (RC) network, examines the capacitor voltage at one time constant, and applies the same charging behaviour to a power-on-reset circuit for an active-low microcontroller.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When a capacitor $C$ is charged through a series resistor $R$ from a constant supply $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to
\[V(t) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)\]
where $V_0$ is the supply voltage, $R$ is the resistance, and $C$ is the capacitance. The product $RC$ is called the time constant,
\[\tau = RC\]
After one time constant, the capacitor reaches approximately 63.2\% of the supply voltage.

\section{Voltage at the Time Constant}

Starting from the charging equation,
\[V(t) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right)\]
and substituting $t = \tau$,
\[V(\tau) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-\tau/RC}\right)\]
Since $\tau = RC$,
\[V(\tau) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-RC/RC}\right)\]
\[V(\tau) = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-1}\right)\]
\[V(\tau) \approx 0.632V_0\]
For $V_0 = 5.0$ V,
\[V(\tau) \approx 0.632 \times 5.0\]
\[V(\tau) \approx 3.16\text{ V}\]
\section{Power On Reset with an Active Low Reset}

A power-on-reset circuit can be used to hold a microcontroller in reset while the supply voltage is first turning on. In this circuit, the capacitor charges through the resistor. The active-low reset pin remains low while the capacitor voltage is below the input-high threshold $V_{IH}$. Once the capacitor voltage crosses $V_{IH}$, the reset is released and the microcontroller can begin operating.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/drawn/por_circuit.pdf}
\caption{RC power-on-reset network driving an active-low reset pin.}
\label{fig:por}
\end{figure}

The reset pin releases when the node voltage reaches $V_{IH}$. Starting from
\[V_{IH} = V_0 \left(1 - e^{-t_{\mathrm{release}}/RC}\right)\]
divide by $V_0$:
\[\frac{V_{IH}}{V_0}=1-e^{-t_{\mathrm{release}}/RC}\]
Rearranging,
\[e^{-t_{\mathrm{release}}/RC}=1-\frac{V_{IH}}{V_0}\]
Taking the natural logarithm,
\[-\frac{t_{\mathrm{release}}}{RC}=\ln\left(1-\frac{V_{IH}}{V_0}\right)\]
Therefore,
\[t_{\mathrm{release}}=-RC\ln\left(1-\frac{V_{IH}}{V_0}\right)\]
Solving for $R$ gives
\[R =\frac{-t_{\mathrm{release}}}{C\ln\left(1-\frac{V_{IH}}{V_0}\right)}\]
For the worked example, let
\[V_{IH} = 0.7V_0\]
with $V_0 = 5.0$ V, $t_{\mathrm{release}} = 10$ ms, and $C = 100$ nF.
\[\ln(1-0.7)=\ln(0.3)\approx -1.204\]
Therefore,
\[R =\frac{-(10\times10^{-3})}{(100\times10^{-9})(-1.204)}\]
\[R \approx 83\,\text{k}\Omega\]
An $83\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor with a $100$ nF capacitor gives a reset hold-off of approximately 10 ms.
